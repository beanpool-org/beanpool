This page says what BeanPool keeps about you, where, who can see it, for how long, and what you can do about it. It also says plainly what we cannot protect you from.

BeanPool is open-source software, not one company's server. Each community runs on its own server, and whoever runs it (its operator) looks after that community's data. BeanPool itself runs a few shared services (section 1). The short version

  • Your account is a key made on your own device. Your 12 words are the only way back that depends on nobody.
  • Your community's operator can read what their server holds, except your direct messages and group chats, which are end-to-end encrypted.
  • Other members can't see your balance or trades. On a local community only its members see its listings.
  • The apps contain no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting code.
  • If you link a Google, Apple or Facebook sign-in, a locked copy of your account is kept in BeanPool's key vault in Iceland, which we open only to give it back to you, or under a valid court order [LAWYER].

1. Who runs what

Communities. Anyone can run one. We can't log in to the servers of communities we don't run; their operators decide how that data is looked after, under their own law, so ask them first. [LAWYER: is BeanPool responsible for strangers' communities as the software's publisher, the registrar that names them, or the directory that lists them?]

If a community uses a beanpool.org address, that name, and any tunnel our address service made for it, is in BeanPool's Cloudflare account, even for a community we don't run. Everything that reaches the community through that address passes through Cloudflare, which decrypts it on the way. So we, or anyone who took over that account, could point the name elsewhere or read or change that traffic. A community on its own domain is not affected.

What BeanPool runs: the global community (global.beanpool.org; we are its operator; no Beans there); a few local communities in Australia we also operate [MARTY: name them or not]; the key vault (section 3); the address service (names such as yourtown.beanpool.org) and its control-room alerts (section 11); the communities directory; this website with its newsletter and the members' guide; the feedback service (off until the release); and the apps on Google Play and the App Store.

2. Your key and your 12 words

Your account is a key made on your device. It signs everything you do. Your 12 words rebuild it.

On a phone the key and words live in the phone's secure storage, set to stay on that phone: not in its backups, so a new phone needs the 12 words, a linked sign-in (section 3), or an admin's re-key code (below). The app asks for the phone's fingerprint or face unlock before showing the words or letting the account leave the phone; with only a PIN or no lock it does not ask. App Lock in Settings adds a lock of its own. Screenshots are blocked while the words show. Copy puts the words on the clipboard; the app clears it after a minute, but other apps or a synced clipboard may read them first. Print or save as PDF makes a recovery kit that goes wherever you send it: that file is yours to guard.

In a web browser the key is kept in that browser's storage on your computer, which is not hardware-protected. The web app's code comes from the server of the community you open: on global, from us. Whoever runs that server could change that code to read a key used there (section 16).

Your key leaves your device only when you choose: linking another device (sent to it encrypted), a locked sign-in copy (section 3), or your own Copy or Print.

Lost everything? In a community that allows it, an admin can give you a one-time re-key code. Your new phone proposes itself with the code and shows two digits; the admin types them in front of you, and your account moves to the new key. The old key is refused from then on. The community keeps a record that the key changed and who re-keyed whom; the code itself is never written to the log. The same person who runs the server could re-key any account this way: that is why the names list and your 12 words are what protect you, not the server (section 16).

3. Signing in with Google, Apple or Facebook

On a local community a sign-in is optional: a way back if you lose your phone. On the global community you choose at the door: 12 words alone (nothing about you reaches the community but your key, your name and, if you give it, your rough area) or one sign-in. A 12-words account starts with smaller daily limits for 7 days and until 3 kept posts; a sign-in account for 72 hours and 3 posts (section 4). Each sign-in account can have one account on global; a sign-in can be added later.

What a sign-in shares. The provider tells BeanPool it's you, with an account number and usually your email. The email is shown to you shortened (m•••@gmail.com) and never stored. The provider learns you signed in to BeanPool. Global keeps a fingerprint of the account (a keyed code over the provider and account number; the key is a file beside the database, never in a copy of it), so one sign-in means one member. It is erased when you delete your account, unless the community had suspended or removed you.

The locked copy. When you link a sign-in, the app locks a copy of your key (and your words, if the phone has them) to that sign-in and sends it to BeanPool's key vault, a small server in Iceland that does nothing else. [MARTY: the release build points at the live vault only once the custodian reshare is done; until then the copy stays at your community, where its operator can open it. State which the release ships.] The vault keeps the copy, which BeanPool account and which kind of sign-in it belongs to, your devices' notification addresses (so it can warn them), two keyed lookup codes, and the day it last changed. It keeps no names, emails, IP addresses or communities.

Getting back in. The vault checks your sign-in with the provider itself, then waits 24 hours unless a device that still has your account taps Yes, it's me. Your devices are told at once and can tap Stop. The record of a restore is deleted 7 days after it ends.

Who can open a copy. The vault can open every copy it keeps, because it checks your sign-in itself. Running it takes two of its three custodians, who live in different countries [MARTY: say this only once the reshare is done; today one person holds all three shares]. The hosting company can read the running machine's memory and so could open any copy. A court could order us to (section 15). Anyone who takes over your Google, Apple or Facebook account could get in unless you Stop it within the 24 hours: protect that account with its own strong password and two-step login.

If you want nobody but you to ever get in, keep your 12 words safe and tap Disconnect next to each sign-in in Settings: your copy goes from the vault at once and from its backups within 30 days. Copies made by older versions sat on your community's server, where its operator could open them; the app offers to move yours and deletes the old one. On [MARTY: the V5 date] the communities we run delete every old copy still left.

4. What your community holds, and who sees it

Your community's server holds your profile (name, picture, bio, contact details), your listings, events, polls and photos (location and camera data stripped from every photo), your messages, your balance and trades, your groups, the reports and blocks you make, your votes and your settings. On global, your rough area if you gave one.

Other members see your profile; your trust profile (trades finished and cancelled, how many people you've dealt with, Trust Points); reviews and who wrote each; who invited you and whom you invited; and what you share with them. Your contact details are hidden until you choose who sees them. Nobody else sees your balance or trades, except: the two people in a trade; a review (which shows you traded); a Decision about you, to its voters, inside that Decision; and, in a known community that confirms members by real name, the admins, under the consent you give when you join (section 8). Any admin can see unfinished or admin-settled trades and the fraud alerts that name you, each look logged. Your real name in a known community is sealed on the admins' phones: the server and BeanPool cannot read it. A poll is anonymous unless its maker chose an open vote; on global a poll also shows how many votes came from new or 12-words accounts.

People who aren't members of a local community see a sign-in page instead of its listings. Still public to anyone who opens its address: its name and member count; its enterprises, their pins, who keeps each and the Beans pledged; crowdfunds, Commons projects and Decisions, with who started each (a Decision can name a member); its Pulse feed; profile pictures and the name behind any member key; whether a name is in use; a listing's photos to anyone with the link. On global, visitors see only listings (photos, a rough area, no names), basic details, and whether a name is in use.

Your community's operator can read everything the server holds except the content of direct messages and group chats. That includes event and enterprise chats, every balance and trade, who reported or blocked whom, and every vote. Who runs the server can also re-key any account and make any key an admin (section 2). A standby, if the community keeps one, copies nearly everything, and whoever runs it can read that copy. The hosting company can reach the disk and memory of any server it rents out.

Messages. Direct messages, and their photos, are end-to-end encrypted: the server stores only what the two of you can read, and refuses a readable one. Group chats are end-to-end encrypted on the phone and, from this release, on the web: the server stores only sealed lines. It does see who wrote, when, and who was mentioned; it reads the system lines (joins, leaves, a vote or event posted); a newcomer learns nothing from before they joined; when someone leaves, the group's key changes. Lines written before a chat was locked were removed. Event and enterprise chats are not encrypted: the operator can read them, and the app says so. The server also reads the notices it adds to any chat about a deal (Beans held, paid or returned, and how a stuck trade was settled), emoji reactions, and a message the operator sends you. Blocking someone stops their messages and notifications on the server: what they write to you is kept for them alone, never delivered, and the operator can see who blocked whom.

New accounts on global have daily limits (posts, photos, new people messaged) and reported posts are hidden when enough independent members report them, for a moderator to review; repeat offenders are muted; the team moderates. Moderators also see which accounts joined from the same connection within a day, as a label, never the address.

Linked communities are off unless an operator turns linking on. A listing stays in your community unless you widen its reach; a member of a linked community you deal with gets a visitor row there, with your name.

5. Location

BeanPool does not track your location: no background location, and the phone apps ask for it only while you use them.

Location exists as a pin you place on a listing, enterprise or event (your device's location once, or by hand). A listing's pin is rounded to about 11 m; an enterprise or event pin is saved where you put it unless you mark it Approximate (about 100 m). Listing and event pins are seen by your community's members; enterprise pins by anyone who opens a local community's map; on global, visitors see a rough area only.

Your rough area (global; optional elsewhere): rounded to about 11 km before it is stored, read back only by you, used to pick the listings nearest you and to decide which nearby phones hear about a new listing. Others see only how many kilometres away you are. A listing you post on global with no pin takes your rough area as its place, visible to every reader including visitors. Places you ask to be alerted about are stored rounded the same way.

6. Notifications

If you allow them, your phone gives the app a notification address, which the server keeps locked. Notifications go through Expo's service, then Apple or Google, to your phone. Each carries fixed words only (a kind of event, no name, listing, amount or group), signed by your community; the app then fetches the real text from your own server, where it is kept for 7 days. Expo, Apple and Google see only that your community sent you a notice of that kind. You can switch kinds off in Settings; warnings that someone is getting into your account, or that your server's owners must act, can't be switched off. The vault keeps your devices' notification addresses beside your copy, under its own key.

8. Known Communities: Real Names and What Admins Can See

Some communities confirm their members by real name, the way a LETS keeps a membership list. In such a known community, your name is sealed on the admins' phones before it reaches the community's server: the operator and BeanPool cannot read it. Your balance stays private to you except in the four cases below. What any admin can see of your trades is listed below too, and it is nothing more. The first case is one you agree to when you join: the join screen says, in your community's own numbers, when the admins can see your balance (if it goes past a share of your credit line, or if you stay in debit for a number of days without a sale).

  • Only with your consent, and only once confirmed: the app records when you agreed and to which wording. Without that, or in a community that does not confirm members, the admins' list of balances never shows you. Saying no changes nothing else.
  • You can take your consent back at any time, in Settings, as easily as you gave it. From that moment you are off the list. The app keeps a record that you agreed and that you withdrew.
  • When an admin removes your account: the admin who starts removing you sees your balance and how many trades you have open, so that what you hold or owe can be settled. This happens whether or not you agreed to anything above.
  • When the community votes on removing you: everyone who can vote in it sees your balance and any debt, in that vote only. An admin or an owner who can't vote in it doesn't see them there. Those looks are not logged.
  • The watchers are watched: every time an admin opens the list of balances, the node records who and when; every time an admin sees your balance while removing you, it records who, whose and when. Every other admin and the owner can see that record. A look at your balance in a vote on removing you is not recorded. There is no export of balances.
  • The server's operator: whoever runs your community's server holds its whole database, balances and trades included, and its backups, snapshots and standby copies, as the operator of any server does.
  • What any admin can see of trades, in any community and whatever you agreed to: a trade that isn't finished yet or that an admin settled (both members, the listing, the price, and the messages in the two members' one-to-one chat, which an admin can't read if it is private), so that a stuck trade can be settled; a trade whose Beans were left stuck when a member was removed on an older server (the trade's status, the listing, the price, its dates, the Beans left stuck, how many payments went through it, and the last one's amount and note); a fraud alert that names two members who buy from each other back and forth, about evenly, past a limit, with the Beans in total and how evenly they went each way; a fraud alert that names a member and the members they invited when those members send them Beans past a limit within a set number of days, with the Beans in total and how many of the members they invited have traded with no one but them; a fraud alert that names a group of members, at least half of them new, who trade mostly with each other, with how much of the group's trading is with each other but no Beans; and an alert that names the members who have had no Beans move in or out for a set number of days. Every look at one of those trades is logged, with who looked, when, and at which trades; a look at the alerts that name a member is logged the first time each admin opens them, and again at that admin's first look after 24 hours, and the looks in between add no line. The owner and the admins can see that log. The member stats the admins see show how many posts each member has up and messages they have sent, and of trades only the whole community's totals, never one member's. Every member, admins included, sees each member's trust profile: how many of their trades were finished and how many were cancelled, and the share finished, how many Bean payments they have sent to or received from members plus the trades they have finished, with how many different members they have paid, been paid by or traded with, how many payments and trades they have done with the member looking, and their Trust Points. It isn't logged, because every member can see it. Nothing else of anyone's trades.
  • Who holds it: the community, not BeanPool. Ask your community's admins to see, correct or delete your entry. A record of a debt left behind when a member leaves is kept 3 years, then deleted.

9. The Pulse: channels you connect (TikTok, Instagram and video feeds)

Connecting TikTok or Instagram is optional. Their access tokens stay on your device, this-device-only (not in phone backups), and are deleted on Sign Out or Disconnect. BeanPool asks only public-profile and public-post permissions, never messages or follower lists. When you verify a channel, your phone tells the node your platform username once, to check it matches the channel you added; the node stores the time it was verified.

The node stores each post's public metadata (link, title, thumbnail link, date, category) and a cached preview image (still images only, at most 2 MB each, 100 MB on disk), keeps the 20 newest items per channel, scrubs pruned or deleted items to NULL at once, and never hosts video or audio. Disconnecting clears the token on your device and the verification on the node.

10. Events, RSVPs and event chat

The server stores what you type into an event, its pin and photos, the private note (host and Going members only) and each RSVP. Members marked Going see each other in the event chat, which the operator can read. Thirty days after the event ends the RSVPs, chat and note are deleted from the server and its standby.

13. How long it is kept

  • Your account: until you delete it.
  • Daily snapshots: 7 by default, never more than 14 or older than 14 days. Locked off-box backups (if the operator set a store): 30 days at most; nobody who finds the file can read it. Operator downloads: as long as they keep them.
  • Events' RSVPs, chat and note: 30 days after the end. Knocks: 30 days. Deletion records for copies: 30 days. Activity feed: 30 days.
  • Logs: 30 days or 2,500 lines, and never an IP address or a code; the console output 2 × 5 MB.
  • IP addresses: in memory for rate limits; a keyed hash for one day at global's door (7 days for a newcomer the community removed); the addresses that copy a server, 7 days, then blanked; none survive into any snapshot, backup or standby. A join-cohort label (random, not the address) until you delete.
  • Pulse: 20 items a channel. Known-community debt records: 3 years after leaving.
  • The vault: your copy until Disconnect; backups 30 days; deletion record 30 days; restore record 7 days; error log 7 days.
  • Global's sign-in fingerprint: while the account exists (longer if suspended or removed).
  • Feedback: no fixed schedule yet [MARTY]. Newsletter: until you ask.

14. Your choices

12 words only, or a sign-in (Disconnect removes it); who sees your contact details; Approximate pins and your rough area; which notifications; block a member; Sign out (this device only). A copy of your ledger (the Commons pool, your balance, every transaction you are party to) is yours to fetch. Ask your operator for anything else; for communities we run, email admin@beanpool.org.

Delete your account (Settings → Account Deletion & Sign Out → Permanently Delete Account). At once, on that community: your profile becomes "Deleted Member" with picture, bio, contact, area gone; every post you wrote loses its words, photos and place and is cancelled (a poll keeps its question and votes); every message you wrote, in any chat, reads "This message was deleted" and its photos are deleted; your notification addresses, place alerts, blocks, friends, settings, Pulse channels, any old sign-in copy, and your name and keys in the server's log are removed; you leave any enterprise you keep; any balance is settled with the Commons; the file's free space is emptied. Refused while a deal of yours is in escrow.

What stays: the ledger entries of your trades (other members' balances depend on them); your votes; ratings and reports you gave or got; your invites and who invited you; the groups you were in; a known community's names-list entry (the admins' record) and any debt record, 3 years; the fact a key was re-keyed; copies made before you deleted (snapshots up to 14 days, off-box backups up to 30, an operator's downloads, a stopped standby).

Deleting is per community and takes your account off this phone for all of them: delete at the others first, or keep your 12 words. It does not delete your vault copy: tap Disconnect first [MARTY: or fix so Delete reaches the vault].

16. Security, honestly

What protects you: a key made and kept on your device; direct messages and group chats end-to-end encrypted; photos stripped of metadata; pushes that say nothing; IP addresses that leave within 7 days; backups that leave a server only locked; the vault's 24-hour wait and 2-of-3 custodians.

What we cannot protect you from: your operator, their standby and their host; an operator who re-keys your account or makes a stranger an admin (the names list and your 12 words are the defence, and a known community's admins see the log); Cloudflare and whoever controls our Cloudflare account; the vault's host reading its memory; two custodians, or a court that compels them; whoever can publish the apps or change the web app's code on a server you use; anyone who sees your 12 words. The phone app is the safer place for your key.
