Exchange for Communities,
Not Commercial Banks

BeanPool is a free app your community uses to trade help, goods and skills with each other. Instead of money, you use Beans: help someone and Beans come to you; get help and they go the other way. Zero is the goal — give about as much as you take.

How Beans move

Three neighbours, one week

1
Sam fixes Ana's bike — 30 Beans

Sam goes up to +30. Ana goes down to −30.

2
Ana bakes bread for Lee — 30 Beans

Ana is back to 0. Lee is now at −30.

3
Lee minds Sam's dog — 30 Beans

Everyone is back near zero, and three jobs got done without anyone spending money.

The seller keeps 98.5% of each price; the other 1.5% goes to the community's Commons pool, so the real numbers land a little under these.

What BeanPool is

A community runs BeanPool on its own small server. Members post what they can offer and what they need, and trade in Beans. A Bean is not money you buy: it is a record that someone helped someone. Everyone starts at zero. When you help, your balance goes up; when you are helped, it goes down; across the community the balances always add up to zero. Nobody issues Beans, nobody lends them at interest, and nobody cashes them out. A small fee on each trade goes to the community's own pool, which its members spend by vote. The app is free and open source; a community picks its own server, and the data on it is theirs.

  • Not a cryptocurrency. No blockchain, no token, nothing to buy or sell. Your 12 words only unlock your own account.
  • Not a bank. Credit comes from your own trading, with no interest: your line opens small after your first completed trade and grows as you trade.
  • Not a company in the middle. BeanPool takes nothing from a trade, and shows no ads.

Mutual credit is old: LETS groups ran it on paper for decades, and in Switzerland a circle of businesses, WIR, has traded in its own credit since 1934. Paperwork is what wore most of them out. BeanPool is the paperwork, done by the app.

How It Works

From the first screen to your first trade.

📩
01

1. Come in by one of three doors

Open the invite a member sent you; soon you can also try the global community with no invite and, from there, ask a community near you to let you in. Either way you pick the name people will know you by.

🔑
02

2. Keep your 12 words

The app makes your account on your phone and shows you 12 words. Write them down and keep them safe: they bring your account back if you lose or change your phone.

🧺
03

3. Post an offer

Put up something you can do or share: a lift to town, eggs, fixing a bike, an hour of gardening. In a community with Beans, you need at least one offer up before you can post a need or buy. On the global community, which has no Beans, your first post can be a need.

🤝
04

4. Trade

Agree a price in Beans. The Beans are held safely until the job is done, then they go to the seller. You rate each other afterwards.

What You Can Do

The app opens on Home, a set of cards you choose. Behind it: the Market, the Map, Talk (Chat in the web app), People, the Pulse, the Commons and the Ledger, where you see your balance and your trades.

🧺

Market

What people in your community are offering and what they need, with polls and events in the same feed. Search it, filter by category, and tap to trade.

🗺️

Map

See what is on offer near you. Every pin is one you place: use your location at that moment, or drop the pin by hand. When you allow it, the app also uses your location to show what is nearest you, and never in the background. Event pins, and enterprise pins set in the web app, can be set to Approximate, which rounds the spot to about 100 m. Pins are seen by the members of your community. When the global community opens to everyone, a visitor there sees listings and roughly where they are, but no names, no profile pictures and no exact spots. Anyone can join it in a minute, so its members see each other's listings to about a kilometre too; the exact spot is yours to share when you arrange to meet.

📅

Events New

Post a working bee, a repair café or a skill-share with a time and a place. People tap Going or Interested. People going get the event chat and the host's private note — the gate code, what to bring — and hear about it if the time or place changes or it is cancelled. Where anyone can join, as on the global community, the host lets each person in first, by default. Events are free; no Beans change hands. Find them on the map under Today, This weekend or Next 7 days, and use Copy to a new date to run one again.

💬

Talk (Chat in the web app)

Message anyone in your community. Direct messages and group chats are end-to-end encrypted: your community's node carries them but cannot read them. On a node that hasn't been updated yet, a group chat can still be read by the people who run it, and the chat says so. Enterprise discussions and event chats can be read by the people who run your community's node, so they can be moderated.

🏛️

The Commons: enterprises New

An enterprise is something your community runs together: an egg flock, a tool library, a firewood depot, or a project with a goal and an end date. Keepers run it and a lead keeper leads it; members can ask to join as keepers. It has its own Beans, posts offers and needs in the Market like anyone else, shows its income and spending, and can pause for the season. No keeper can approve a payment to themselves: another keeper has to.

🗳️

Polls and Decisions New

Anyone can ask the community a question with a poll in the Market. Bigger choices are Decisions, in the Commons tab: a grant from the Commons pool to an enterprise, a hardship grant, replacing a lead keeper. A Decision is open for 7 days and carries itself out if it passes. Once you have completed a trade, you can propose one.

👥

Groups New

Start a group for your street, your garden club or your band, and you become its convenor. Post to the group only, and choose whether it is open, people ask to join, or it is invite only.

📡

The Pulse

See what local people are making. Members can connect their own channels — TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, SoundCloud or a blog feed — and their public posts show up in your community's Pulse. Connecting is up to each member. Anything that shouldn't be there can be reported, and your community's node can take it down.

Credit You Earn, Not Borrow

Nobody buys Beans, and nobody pays interest.

0️⃣

Everyone starts at zero

A new account has 0 Beans. When you sell, your balance goes up; when you buy, it goes down. Across the whole community the balances always add up to zero.

⚖️

Your first trade opens a credit line

Once you have completed a trade, you can start to go below zero to get help before you have given it back. Your credit line starts small (a 100-Bean trade earns about 37 Beans of it) and grows as you trade with more people. The offers you keep up set how much of it you can use: one offer up to 200 Beans, five offers up to 2000.

🏅

Badges, not gates

Over time you earn badges: Newcomer, Resident, Steward, Elder. They show your history in the community. They don't unlock anything. An admin can also give someone a badge, and with it that badge's starting credit line.

Where the fees go

The value stays in your community

A 1.5% fee on completed trades, and a small monthly fee on the part of a balance above 200 Beans, go to your community's Commons pool — not to us or anyone else. Your community decides what the pool pays for, through Decisions.

Your Account and Your Safety

🔑

Your key stays on your phone

On the phone app your key is held in the phone's secure storage, and you can add App Lock, which asks for your phone's own screen lock. In the web app it is held in that browser's own storage. Your 12 words are the backup that depends on nobody. You can also link a Google, Apple or Facebook sign-in as a second way back. A locked copy of your key is then kept for that sign-in. Phone apps that use BeanPool's key vault keep it there, on a small server in Iceland that does nothing else; older versions of the phone app keep it on your community's node; the web app on the global community keeps it on that community's server, which we run. Whoever keeps the copy can open it: BeanPool for the vault and the global community, the people who run your node for a copy kept there. A restore from the vault waits a day unless one of your own devices says it's you. That copy, and copying your account to another device of yours, are the only ways your key leaves your device.

🚩

Report anything

You can report a post, a member, an event or a Pulse item, and block anyone. Reports go to the people who run your community's node — its owner and admins — who can remove content and accounts. Members can also vote on a Decision to suspend or remove someone.

Run BeanPool for Your Community

Every community runs on its own node: a small server that holds that community's members, posts and ledger. Someone local runs it. If your town or valley doesn't have one yet, it could be you.

🧰

What you look after

You and the admins you appoint are your community's moderators: reports come to you. Your members' data lives on your machine, so you keep it backed up. You can read enterprise discussions and event chats; you can't read direct messages. You invite the first members, and they invite the rest.

⚙️

The settings app

Your node comes with its own settings app in the browser. From it you name your community, appoint an owner and admins who sign in with their own keys, print QR invites, watch disk health, choose a public web address or a tunnel, and help a member who has lost both their phone and their 12 words back into their own account on a new phone, with a one-time code you confirm in person.

🔒

Private from the first boot

A community's listings, map, messages and balances are for its members from the moment its node starts; there is nothing to switch on. Someone who opens its address without an account is pointed to the global community, which opens to everyone soon.

🌐

Linking with a neighbouring community

Two communities can agree to link so their members can trade with each other. Beans never leave home: each pair keeps a running tally of what one community owes the other, and each side sets its own limit. Linking is off unless both operators switch it on, and it is still early: it is built, with its screens in the web app, and has been tried only between two of our own test communities, never between live ones.

Start a community

About half an hour, if you can follow a recipe. Four things to know first:

  • What it costs. The software is free. It runs on a spare computer that stays on at home (a Mini PC, a NAS, or a Raspberry Pi with a 64-bit system), or on a small rented server for a few dollars a month (about US$6).
  • Who hosts it. You do, on your own machine or the one you rent. It never needs beanpool.org: your own web address works.
  • What you take on. Reports from your members come to you. The data folder is yours to back up, with a recovery code. Keep two owners. Keep the server on and updated.
  • What you never see. Your members' direct messages.

Then, on that machine with Docker installed, paste this one block. It fetches the compose file, creates the network the node uses, pulls the published image (Intel/AMD or ARM64), starts it, waits until the server has made its one-time claim code (up to five minutes, for a slow first start on a Raspberry Pi), and claims it. There is nothing to configure before it starts; Docker may warn that some settings are not set, and that is expected.

bash — start a community
One block: fetch, network, pull, start, wait, claim git clone https://github.com/beanpool-org/beanpool.git && cd beanpool docker network create beanpool-shared docker compose pull docker compose up -d echo "Waiting for the server to make its claim code (up to 5 minutes)..." for i in $(seq 60); do docker compose exec -T beanpool-node test -f /data/claim-code.txt </dev/null 2>/dev/null && break; sleep 5; done docker compose exec -T beanpool-node test -f /data/claim-code.txt </dev/null 2>/dev/null || echo "No claim code after 5 minutes. The server's log says why: docker compose logs beanpool-node" docker compose exec beanpool-node beanpool claim

The last line: beanpool claim

  1. Your address. It asks for the first part of your address (type just yourtown for yourtown.beanpool.org; Enter skips) and waits up to three minutes for it to go live. For your own domain, run it with --address https://your.domain instead; to choose later in Settings, --no-name, and add --direct http://IP:PORT (this server's own address) so the QR code carries it, or the phone asks for it. If the BeanPool project's address service is down, the claim still works.
  2. The code and QR. It shows a one-time claim code and a QR code. Scan the QR code with your phone's camera, or open BeanPool, type the server's address under Find your community and tap Claim. That phone becomes the owner.
  3. Settings. Open your address (or the server's own address) followed by /settings. The setup wizard names your community, asks you to add a second owner, and makes three founding invites. Set your place on the map under Node Identity so people nearby can find you.

If the block says No claim code after 5 minutes, or beanpool claim says This community has no claim code waiting, the server has not finished starting: docker compose logs beanpool-node shows where it is. Once the log says the community has no owner yet and has a claim code, run the last line again. The full steps are in the operator manual's first-time setup.
