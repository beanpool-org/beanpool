## What you can report

- **A post in the Market:** open it and tap **Report Post** (in the web app, **Report**).
- **An event:** open it and tap **Report event**.
- **A Pulse post:** tap the flag on it.
- **A project or an enterprise:** open it and tap **Report Project** or **Report Enterprise** at the bottom.
- **A message in a chat:** in a group's chat or a chat with one person, on the phone, tap the message, then the flag (**Report message**). In the web app, tap **Report** under it. You can report anybody's message but your own.
- **A message request:** tap **Report** on the request. This also blocks the person who sent it. If they have deleted the message, or your community doesn't take it, your report of them goes without its words. They are still blocked.

Then:

Choose a reason (**Spam or scam**, **Offensive content**, **Misleading post**, **Child safety** or **Other**), add a few words if there is a box for them, and tap **Submit report** (**Submit Report** in the web app). Every report screen offers the same reasons, in the phone app and the web app.

**Child safety** is for a child at risk, or sexual content involving a minor. Choose it whenever that is what you saw, even if another reason fits too: the people who run your community's server are told at once and look at it first. Their alert says only that a report came in. In the Reports queue, your community's moderators see it as they see any report: the reason you chose, any words you added (the phone leaves your words out when it knows what you reported as a private chat, or can't read which chats here are private, and the **Reported** message says so), and that it came from you. If the words you added look like a report about a child's safety, the report goes as **Child safety** even though the words stay out, and the message says that too. Don't count on it: choose **Child safety** yourself. Nobody else is told who reported. If you already reported it with another reason, report it again and choose **Child safety**. If a child is in danger right now, call your local emergency number first.

## A person or a message

A group's chat is end-to-end encrypted, so your community's moderators can't read it. When you report a message there, your app sends that one message's own key with the report. With it the moderators can open that message, its photo if it has one, and see who sent it, and check that it really is theirs: a message someone changed, or one made up, shows them **Could not be verified**. The key opens nothing else in the chat. The report box tells you what goes. You can report each message once. The one exception is **Child safety**: if a moderator has already dealt with your report and you then choose **Child safety**, it goes to them as a new report.

A chat with one person is end-to-end encrypted too, but it has no key for one message: the only key opens the whole chat, so your app never sends it. When you report a message there, your app sends that message still sealed, its sender's signature, and the words as you read them. The moderators can check who sent that sealed message to you, even if it is deleted afterwards. They can't check the words, so they read them as your account of it. A photo stays sealed. Nothing else in the chat goes with the report.

To report a person in the phone app, rather than a message, block them: blocking also sends a report to your community's moderators. See "Blocking someone".

In the web app, a chat with one person has a report button at the top: a flag, with the word **Report** on a wide screen. A member's profile in the web app has one too: the flag at the top, or **Report Member** lower down. Both use the same reasons.

## What happens next

Your report goes to your community's moderators: the owners and admins who run its server, and any members they have made moderators. They decide what to do. A moderator can remove a post or a Pulse post. Only the owners and admins can suspend a member, and where the community votes, it then votes on removing them.

- **A Market post or an event:** if they remove it, you get an alert: "The post you reported was removed". If they look at it and keep it, you are told that too. If its author took it down before they looked, you are told it is no longer up.
- **A Pulse post, a project, an enterprise, a person or a message in a group's chat:** you are not told the outcome in the app.
- The moderators see who sent each report. Nobody else is told who reported: not the person you reported, and not anyone else who reported the same thing.

These alerts come under **Marketplace Activity** in your notification settings. On your phone's lock screen each one says only "Your community has a notice for you."; open BeanPool to read it. See "Notifications". The web app has no notifications: it shows each alert while it is open, or the next time you open it.

The owners and admins are told when any report comes in: only its kind and how many are waiting, never who sent it or what it is about. Reports that follow soon after are told together as one line.

If one of your own posts is removed by the moderators, you get an alert saying so, and why if they gave a reason. It does not say who reported it.

## A false accusation in a vote about you

If your community is voting on removing you and the case against you is false, open **Commons**, then **Decide**, and tap **Report this case as false** on the vote. The report goes to the owners and admins like any other, and names whoever brought the case. You can also reply to the case on the vote itself, where everyone reads it beside the case. If the case is shown to be false, an admin can hide it, with a reason everyone can read in its place. See "Rules and how decisions work".

Now and then the admins clear out old listings to keep the Market tidy. If some of yours are cleared, you get one alert saying how many. That is routine tidying, not a report.

## On global.beanpool.org

The global community at global.beanpool.org is open to anyone, with 12 secret words or a sign-in, so it looks after itself a little more:

- **A post reported by three members who don't know each other** is hidden at once, until a moderator looks at it. Each of them must have been a member for at least a week, be past the new-account limits below themselves, and be at least half as established there as the person who posted it. Time as a member counts most, then posts that stayed up and people they have finished a deal with. Trust badges don't count. People who joined from the same internet connection within a day of each other count as one, and so do people who came in on invites from the same person. Someone who reported three posts in a month that the moderators then kept doesn't count for a while. Otherwise the reports wait for the moderators, as they do everywhere. So a few new accounts can't hide the posts of someone who has been there for months.
- **A post by a new account made with 12 words** is hidden by one such report, while the account is on its new-account limits. Once it is past them, or has added a sign-in, its posts need three like anyone's.
- **A hidden post** can still be seen by its author, who gets an alert that it is hidden, not removed. If the moderators keep it, everyone can see it again.
- **Accounts that joined together.** The moderators can see which accounts joined from the same internet connection within a day of a reported one, never the connection itself, so they can deal with a burst of fake accounts in one go: hide all their posts while they look, or remove the accounts. If yours are hidden that way, you get one alert, "Your posts are hidden for review", and they come back if the moderators keep them. People in one household or at one meetup often join together, so the moderators check each account before they act.
- **New accounts have daily limits** for their first 3 days, and until 3 of their posts have stayed up: 3 posts, 5 photos, and 10 new people to message in any 24 hours. An account made with 12 words has smaller ones, for its first 7 days and until 3 of its posts have stayed up: 2 posts, 4 photos and 3 new people. Adding a sign-in moves it to the usual ones at once, counted from when it joined. Replying to someone who wrote to you first is never limited. If you reach a limit, the app says which one and when it lets up.
- **Why new accounts start slowly.** Anyone can make an account there, and nothing stops one person making many. Behind a sign-in there is a Google, Apple or Facebook account, which takes some effort to make; 12 words take none. So what keeps the global community usable is how little a brand-new account can do, and how quickly its spam can be hidden. Nothing is locked: a new account can do everything an older one can, a little at a time at first, and the limits end once it has been there a while and 3 of its posts have stayed up.
- **If the moderators remove three of your posts within 30 days,** you can't post or send messages there until a moderator lifts it. You can still read, edit your profile and leave.
- **There are no community votes there.** When an admin suspends someone, the suspension lasts 7 days and then lifts by itself, unless an admin lifts it sooner.
- **Polls there are informal.** Each one says "An informal poll; it decides nothing". Anyone can join, with 12 words or a sign-in, so one person with several accounts could tip the count.

None of this happens in your own local community unless its owners turn it on: by default, every report there waits for the moderators. See "How this community works" to check which rules yours has.

If someone is in danger, contact your local emergency services first.
