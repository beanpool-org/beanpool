The community governs itself through Decisions. Members propose and vote in the app. Settings is where owners and admins watch them, and step in when something goes wrong.

## How a Decision runs

- Voting is open for **7 days**. When it closes, the server counts the votes within a minute and, if it passed, carries it out by itself. No admin has to press anything.
- Enough people must vote: at least 3, and at least 30% of members active in the last 30 days (25% for removing someone, where everyone decides it; a jury has its own numbers, see Who decides a removal).
- It passes with 60% yes. Removing someone needs 66% and at least 3 yes votes, whatever the share (a jury's: two-thirds of the votes cast and at least 6 yes of 12); restoring someone needs just over half. A tie fails.
- Votes are secret: members and admins see only the totals. See Privacy and what your server can see, for what the server itself keeps.
- Each member can have one open Decision at a time. Frozen and suspended members cannot propose, and cannot vote, with one exception: someone suspended in an emergency still votes on every other Decision while the community votes on removing them, and counts in its turnout. Nobody votes on a Decision to remove them.

What a passed Decision can do by itself: suspend or restore a member, freeze or unfreeze, remove an enterprise's lead keeper, and pay grants from the commons. A grant the commons cannot yet afford waits in a queue; only one can wait at a time, and it fails after 90 days.

Two other kinds of vote are not the community's. The keepers of an enterprise vote among themselves to pay Beans out of it, to remove a keeper, or to make another keeper the lead (see Enterprises, keepers and the commons). The members of a chat group vote on its lead convenor, its convenors, removing someone from the group, who may join, and closing it. Each runs 7 days, ends early once the result cannot change, and needs more than half of the people on its list to vote. They do not count towards a member's one open Decision. Settings does not show them, and **Halt this Decision** does not reach them: owners and admins hold no power over a group.

## Watching them

**Shared Projects & Economy**, then **Proposals**, shows open votes and removals that are waiting out their grace period, with the totals so far. For a removal a jury is deciding, it shows how many jurors have voted, never how, until the vote closes.

On the global community there are no community votes. There the list shows emergency suspensions, each with the day it lifts and no vote totals, under the heading **Suspensions**. A suspension made while votes were switched off stays one nobody votes on, and shows that way, even if votes are switched back on before it ends.

## Emergency suspension

For when someone is doing harm now. Open the person (People & Safety, then Members) and choose **Suspend**. Write a reason of at least 10 characters: every member will see it.

- They are suspended at once and their role, if any, is set aside.
- While it lasts, their listings are off the Market and the map, and they bring nobody in: no invite, no answer to a request to join, and an invite or paper ticket they made earlier admits nobody. They can still answer in their chats with one person (not in a group's, an event's or an enterprise's chat) and finish or cancel the deals they have open. Their listings and invites come back as they were when it ends.
- A suspension made when acting on a report is the same suspension, and the same vote opens, with the reason given, or that a report about them was upheld.
- A 7-day vote opens on removing them: "Remove …?". It is decided as any removal is (see Who decides a removal): by everyone, one member one vote, 25% turnout, 66% and at least 3 yes votes to remove, or, in a large community, by a jury. They cannot vote on it, and still vote on every other Decision, so suspending someone cannot swing another vote.
- If it does not pass, or too few vote, the suspension lifts when the vote closes and their role comes back. Where fewer than 3 members could vote at all, an owner may decide it (see Too few to vote).
- If it passes, they stay suspended through a removal's 7 days' grace, and are then removed (see Removing someone).
- No vote removes an owner or admin: on one of them, the suspension ends when the vote closes, whatever it decides, and their role comes back.
- A "Keep the suspension?" vote opened before this version runs to its end as it did.
- You cannot suspend yourself, an enterprise, or the last owner. Only an owner can suspend an owner.
- The vote names who suspended them: members read your name on it, never your key.
- The suspended person is told at once: an alert on their phone, and a notice the web app shows when they open it. They can reply to the case on the vote itself, and everyone who reads the vote sees the reply beside it. If they delete their account, the reply goes with it, and an admin can take down a reply that harms someone (see Taking down a reply). Everyone who can vote on it gets an alert too, which never says who it is about. Where a jury decides, only the jurors get one, saying they were drawn.
- **The cool-down:** if a vote on removing someone you suspended ends without removing them (it fails, too few vote, or it is halted or lifted before it closes), you can't suspend them again for 90 days, from their page or from a report. Settings says until when. Another admin can, once the first 30 days are over (below). A removal the community passed that an admin then halted doesn't count.
- **Again within 30 days, an owner, with a new reason:** after a vote on removing someone ends without removing them, only an owner can suspend them again in the next 30 days, and the reason can't be the one the last suspension gave. Settings says until when. The new vote says so to members: "Second pause in 30 days", and how the last vote ended. A removal the community passed that an admin then halted doesn't count.
- **Not a third time in 90 days:** a third suspension of the same person within 90 days of the first is refused to everyone, owners too. If the community has more to decide, a member proposes a Decision, for example to suspend them.
- **The owners are told of every suspension,** on the alert channel that tells them of removals: how many in the day, and whether an admin or an owner made them, never who was suspended or why. A vote's card says who suspended whom.
- **More than five at once:** an admin with five suspensions whose votes are still open makes another only with an owner's fresh proof. An admin asks an owner to make it; an owner presses Manage on the phone. Suspensions that fail before the proof is asked, for another reason, don't use it.
- None of these apply on the global community, where there is no vote, except that the owners are told.
- In their groups, a suspended convenor can use none of a convenor's tools until the suspension ends, and a suspended member joins no group. They stay a convenor, and a group's lead convenor stays its lead convenor: suspending someone never moves a group to anyone else.

On the global community no vote opens. The suspension lasts 7 days and then lifts by itself, and their role comes back, unless you lift it sooner. To keep someone out for longer, suspend them again, or remove their account. The suspended person gets no notification there: tell them yourself.

## Hiding a false case

When the case on a vote on removing someone is shown to be false, **Hide case** on that vote (open, in its grace period, or under **Closed votes on removing a member**) hides it. Write a reason of at least 10 characters: members read it in the case's place. Who brought the case and the member's reply stay; Settings keeps the case and who hid it. A case is hidden once and stays hidden.

Any owner or admin can hide a case, except the one the vote is about. No vote removes an owner or admin, so their role comes back when the vote closes, but they still can't hide its case, just as they couldn't vote on it. Another owner or admin can.

Hiding doesn't stop the vote. To stop it, halt it (below), which lifts the suspension.

The member the vote is about can report the case as a false accusation from the vote. It arrives in Reports like any other, about whoever brought the case. If they already had a report waiting about that person, the case is added to it.

## Taking down a reply

Everyone who reads a vote on removing someone reads the member's reply beside the case. When a reply harms someone else, for example it gives their address or phone number or threatens them, **Take down reply** on that vote (open, in its grace period, or under **Closed votes on removing a member**) takes it down. Settings shows you the reply first. Write a reason of at least 10 characters: members read it in the reply's place.

- The reply is deleted, not hidden: nobody can read it again, Settings included, and your standby drops it at its next copy. A snapshot or backup made before still holds it until it is replaced.
- The node keeps when, which admin, and why. Members read the reason, never who.
- The member is told: an alert on their phone, and a notice the web app shows. Neither says why or who; the vote does. They can't write another reply on that vote.
- The vote itself carries on, and the case stays as it is. A reply is taken down once.
- Owners and admins can take a reply down. Moderators can't.

## Removing someone

Only the community removes a member, by a Decision, and that vote opens only when someone is suspended (above): nobody proposes one, member or admin. A removal a member proposed before this version still runs to its end. When it passes, the person is suspended and frozen at once, and has **7 days' grace**. After that they are removed automatically, and their balance or debt goes to the commons. In those 7 days, **Offboard** on their page settles their beans as you choose (to the commons, or to a member) and completes the removal at once.

Three exceptions. Where fewer than 3 members could vote at all, an owner may decide the vote (see Too few to vote). An owner can still remove a member together with everyone they invited (**Prune Branch**, see Members and invites), until a branch can be put to one vote. And where community votes are switched off, the admins remove members themselves (**Prune Account** and **Offboard**).

No Decision can remove or suspend an owner or admin of your server, one whose role a suspension has set aside included: the app refuses it when someone proposes it, and one that was already open is stopped before it is carried out. The people who run the server cannot be voted out of it, not even by the vote that opens when one of them is suspended (above). If an admin must go, an owner takes the role away first; after that they are a member like any other.

## Who decides a removal

When you suspend someone, a 7-day vote opens on removing them. Who votes on it is a rule of your community, **Who decides a removal**, with three settings:

- **Automatic** (the default): a jury of members drawn at random once at least 100 members could serve on one, and everyone who can vote until then.
- **Everyone**: every member who can vote decides, however big the community grows (25% turnout, 66% yes, at least 3 yes).
- **A jury**: a jury whatever the size, but only from 36 members who could serve on one. While fewer than 36 could, this setting is held back and a suspension made then is decided by everyone.

An owner sets it under **People & Safety → Triage & Moderation**, in **Rules the server applies**; admins can read it. The row says how many members could serve on a jury today. Every change is logged with who made it and members are told. The setting travels with the community's other settings to a standby. A community whose owners never chose has Automatic, and one whose owners chose a rule keeps it. The rule is settled at the suspension, from that day's list, and recorded on the vote, so changing it, or the community growing past 100, changes nothing about a vote that is already open. If the list can't seat a whole jury once members are passed over, everyone decides that vote.

### How a jury is drawn

The server draws the jury in the same step that suspends the member, so a jury never exists without its case. The draw is made once; a seat that falls vacant later is filled from the same order.

- **Who could serve.** Every member who could vote then (active, or suspended and held by nothing but this vote) who joined at least 30 days before that day began (UTC). Enterprises and visitors are out, and so is the member the vote is about. Nothing else is asked: not activity, trading or standing. A member whose credit an admin froze is on the list, so freezing a few members and unfreezing them can't shape a jury. So is the admin or owner who suspended, who is passed over when the jury is drawn, so which admin acts moves nothing.
- **How many.** 12 jurors while fewer than 1,000 could serve, 18 from 1,000. 9 of 12 (13 of 18) must vote, or the vote is not decided. Removing takes two-thirds of the votes cast, at least 6 yes (9 of 18), and more yes than no.
- **Which ones.** The list is put in an order by a SHA-256 hash of the member's key, the list and the day. The first members in that order who are not passed over are the jury. The server keeps the list and the jury with the vote, and they are copied to a standby with it, so a restored node never draws again.
- **Passed over,** each time the draw reaches them, the admin or owner who suspended and anyone with a link to the member that the member could not have made alone: someone who blocked them, who reported them or something they posted in the last 90 days, who invited them or was invited by them, who started a chat with them, who completed a deal with them in the last 90 days (bought from them, answered one of their listings or lent them something), or who has a deal open on one of their listings that they asked for. The member's own acts, such as their blocks or a message they sent, pass nobody over, so they can't pick their jury. None of this is ever shown: a member passed over is listed after the vote exactly like one who stepped aside, and while the vote is open is refused in the words of a member who wasn't drawn.

### Seats

- **Told when drawn.** Each juror gets an alert (if their **Direct Messages** alerts are on) that they were drawn, which names nobody, and finds the case in **Commons**, then **Decide**.
- **72 hours to answer.** **Serve**, and then vote, or **Step aside**, with no reason asked. A vote is an answer too, and once a juror has voted they can't step aside, only change their vote. A juror who gives no answer in 72 hours loses the seat, and so does one who leaves, is removed or is suspended after being drawn. The next member in the order who is not passed over is seated and told, and with less than two days of the vote left, told that it closes soon. A juror who moves to a new phone with a Re-Key code keeps the seat under their new key.
- **Reminders.** A juror with no answer is reminded after 48 hours; every juror who hasn't voted is reminded on the vote's sixth day; and every seated juror is told, once, when the member first answers the case. Each respects the member's own alert switch.
- **Nothing extends the 7 days.** If the jury can't be made whole, fewer vote, and if fewer than the 9 (or 13) vote, the suspension lifts when the vote closes. While formal Decisions are switched off on this server nobody can answer a seat or vote, no seat ends and nobody is alerted, and the 48 and 72 hours leave that time out; the vote's own days run on.

### What is seen, and what you hold

- **While it is open,** members and admins see "7 of 12 jurors have voted", never yes or no. A juror sees only their own seat; nobody else on the jury is named. Everyone else sees "Only the jury votes on this."
- **After it closes,** the totals and two lists, **Voted** and **Didn't serve**, by name. They never say how anyone voted or why someone didn't serve.
- **Check the draw.** Any member can tap it on the vote. Their app takes the list of members who could serve, which your server gives to every member, works out its hash and compares it with the one recorded when the vote opened. After the vote closes it also checks that the names listed are the first ones the draw picks. It catches a list or a jury that doesn't match the draw.
- **What it can't catch.** The list, the jury and the votes are rows in your server's database, readable by anyone who can read a copy of its files, as every vote is. An owner with that access who rewrote the list, its record and the jury together would pass the check. Members are trusting you not to. And because the draw is public, a member who runs it can work out who is probably on a jury, before the vote opens or while it is open, though not who was passed over. The admin who suspends chooses the day, and so which day's draw applies: one try a day, on the record.

### What does not change

- Hiding a case, taking down a reply, halting, the cool-down and the limits on repeat suspensions work as for any removal.
- **Decide as owner** (see Too few to vote) is for votes everyone decides. A jury is only used from 36 members who could serve, so no owner decides a jury's vote.
- No vote removes an owner or admin. A jury is still drawn for one, and whatever it says, the suspension ends when the vote closes.
- Restoring someone stays an ordinary Decision, proposed by a member and decided by everyone who can vote.
- A whole invite branch: the server can put an owner's whole invite branch (a member and everyone they invited) to one removal vote, through its owner API. Under a jury rule one jury decides it, drawn for the first member's key. None of the accounts on the list sits on it, and the branch's whole invite tree and anyone linked to any account on the list are passed over. If it passes, every account on the list has one 7-day wait and is removed at its end; No, a tie or too few jurors lifts every suspension. For now the manager has no button for this and the members' apps don't show a branch's vote, so **Prune Branch** (see Members and invites) is still how an owner removes a branch.

## Too few to vote

In a small community a removal needs at least 3 yes votes, so two neighbours can't remove a third: with 7 active members, 3 of the other 6 must vote yes; in a family of four, all three others. When fewer than 3 members, not counting the person suspended, could vote at all, the vote can never reach its turnout, and left alone the suspension lifts when it closes, whatever they think. The vote still runs its 7 days, and its card tells members: "Fewer than 3 members could vote. If nobody else votes, the owner decides when it closes."

- **An owner decides.** In **Shared Projects & Economy**, then **Proposals**, the vote says so and has **Decide as owner**. Only an owner sees it. Write a reason of at least 10 characters: every member reads it on the vote, beside the case. It asks an owner's fresh proof every time: press Manage on the phone. An admin can't decide it, nor can an automation token, and a computer's session is told to do it from the phone. A short reason, a vote enough members can vote on, a vote past its closing time or a vote whose case is hidden is refused before the proof is asked.
- The suspended person is told at once, and can reply to it. The owners are told on their alert channel: how many in the day, by an owner, never who or why.
- If an admin hides the case as one shown to be false, the owner's reason goes from what members read too, since it may repeat the accusation: the vote says an owner decided, and Settings keeps the reason. It changes nothing else: to stop the removal, halt it.
- Once a case is hidden, an owner can't decide that vote, since no member could read why, and **Decide as owner** goes. Left too few, the suspension lifts when the vote closes; halt the vote to lift it sooner.
- **When the vote closes,** if still fewer than 3 could vote, the decision is carried out: the removal's 7 days' grace begins, recorded on the vote as "Decided by the owner: too few members to vote". Any admin can still halt it in that time, with a public reason.
- If more members could vote by the close (a quiet member voting counts them in), they decide, and it ends as any vote does: the card says so as soon as they can. Nor is it carried out if whoever decided is no longer an owner by then. An owner decides a vote once; to undo it before the close, lift the suspension or halt the vote.
- No vote removes an owner or admin, and an owner's decision doesn't either: Settings offers none on one of them.

## Halting a Decision

**Halt this Decision** stops a Decision that is open or in its grace period. You must give a reason of at least 10 characters, and it is public.

- Halting a removal in its grace period restores the person.
- Halting the vote an emergency suspension opened lifts the suspension.
- A suspension nobody votes on (on the global community) has **Lift suspension now** instead. It asks for a reason the same way, and lifts the suspension straight away.
- **Owner only**: if halting would give someone back an owner or admin role, only an owner can do it. The same goes for lifting such a suspension by hand, and for cutting a grace period short.

Halting overrides the community. Use it for a mistake or an abuse of the process, not because you dislike the result, and say why.

## Know this

- A suspension by Decision takes away the person's role for good. An emergency suspension only sets the role aside while its vote is open: if the vote fails, or you lift or halt it first, the role comes back.
- Lifting a suspension by hand is refused while a removal is waiting out its grace period. Halt the removal instead.
