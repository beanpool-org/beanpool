## What it is

You can write to someone who belongs to another BeanPool community, without joining it. Their community and yours pass the messages between you. Your first message is a message request: they choose whether to accept, as with a member of your own community.

It works only where both communities take messages from other communities. Their owners decide. Where yours doesn't, the app shows no address card and no **Someone in another community**.

## Your address card

Your address card is a QR code and a link with your name and your community. Anyone you show it to can send you one message request from their own community.

- **On the phone:** open **Settings**, then **My address card**. Tap **Show my card**, then show the code, or tap **Share my card** to send the link.
- **In the web app:** open **Chat**, then **✉️ New DM**, then **🌐 Someone in another community**. Tap **Show my card**, then **Copy the link** or **Share my card**.

Under the card is a switch: **People in other communities can send me a message request on this phone** (in the web app, **in this browser**). Each phone or browser you use has its own switch. With it off, nobody in another community can reach you there.

## Being found by your name

Without a card, nobody in another community can find you, unless you turn on **People in other communities can find me by name**. It is under your address card, and off until you turn it on. Turning it on also turns on the switch above for this phone or browser.

Then someone in another community who types your exact name, as you use it here, gets your address card, and can send you a message request. Nobody can see a list of names, and nobody can search by part of a name.

## Writing to someone

If they are in your contacts, you need no card: in **Contacts**, tap **Message** beside their name. A chat you have with them opens, or, if you share no community, a new chat across communities. Under your first message it says **They will see this if they take messages from other communities.**

Otherwise, in **Chat**, open **New Message** (the pencil button; in the web app **✉️ New DM**), then **Someone in another community**.

- **Their card:** tap **Scan a card** and point the camera at their card's QR code, or paste the link they sent you under **Paste a card link**, then tap **Read the card**. In the web app, **Scan a card** shows only where the browser can read a QR code.
- **Their name:** under **Someone in another community**, type **Their community's address**, then **Their exact name**, and tap **Find them**. Only people who said yes can be found.
- If nobody answers to that name, or they haven't said yes, you see the same line: **Nobody by that name takes messages here.** It never says which.
- You can look up 10 names a day. A name is up to 60 characters.

Their card shows their name and their community. A name from another community is their own word, never checked here, so beside the community it always says **name not confirmed here**, on the card and in your chat with them. Write **Your first message**, then tap **Send a message request**.

The chat shows in your list of chats with their community under their name. Until their community has it, your message says **Waiting for their community**.

## A request from another community

A request shows who wrote and from which community: **‹name› from ‹community› wants to message you.** They will not know you saw it unless you accept. Tap **Accept** to answer, **Decline** to say no, or **Block**.

You can report a message, as in any chat. See "Reporting a problem" and "Blocking someone".

## What each community keeps

Neither community keeps a copy of these chats. Each message is kept only on the phone or browser that gets it. On the phone, a chat says **Kept on this phone only. Your community keeps no copy.** So a new phone, or one set up from your 12 words, doesn't have your earlier messages in these chats.

Each message is locked on the phone or browser that sends it, for the one person it is written to: neither community can read it. The two communities see that a message passed between you, and when, as your community does for a message inside it.
